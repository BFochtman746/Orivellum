The rules, so it stays good

What makes these work

Clarify gate

  • Three questions, hard ceiling.Trained clarifiers match the best general models with 41% fewer questions by ranking on relevance and answerability.
  • One question per ambiguous facet.Not one per turn. Facets in parallel is what keeps the count down.
  • Ask what would change the output.Reason over candidate results first, then ask the question that splits them.
  • Always give options, never a bare question.A bare question needs expertise the person may not have yet.
  • Always give a free-text slot.Options are a shortcut, not a cage.
  • Show progress.Named facets and a count, so it never feels like an interrogation with no end.
  • Disclose the default.Every question states what happens if you skip it.
  • Answer in-place.Selections become a single clean turn — not three chat messages.

Next steps

  • Two to four. Three is right.Six or more rebuilds the decision paralysis you were removing.
  • Anchor in the last answer.Quote its number, its name, its finding. Generic chips train people to ignore chips.
  • One narrow, one wide, one act.Directional control without a menu.
  • Editable before it sends.A tap that fires an unwanted side quest is worse than a blank composer.
  • Regenerate on every new answer.A repeated chip is a dead chip.
  • Outside the answer.A hairline above them; never mixed into generated text.
  • Never block the composer.Ignoring them must cost nothing.
  • Exactly one recommendation, or none.Two recommendations is no recommendation. When nothing earns it, say why instead of promoting a weak option.
  • A recommendation states its reason.Cheapest unblocked step, or what it unlocks. No reason, no badge.
  • Auto-run is computed, never claimed.Reversible, inside budget, unblocked, no open clarification. Irreversible never runs on its own.
  • Measure the lift.If the recommendation is taken no more often than any other chip, the recommender is decoration.

Do not show either one when

  • The request is clear enough to act on. A gate on an unambiguous ask is friction wearing a governance costume.
  • The work is cheap and reversible — just do it and offer an undo.
  • A run is already mid-flight. Chips interrupt a plan the person is watching.
  • The answer already ends in a checklist. Do not restate it as chips.
  • You would be asking something the corpus, the settings, or memory already answers. Look first, ask second.
